# 10 — People & Skills

## Purpose

The most important chapter in the handbook. Technology adoption fails without organisational change. This chapter covers skills evolution, team structures, hiring vs upskilling, change management, and the cultural shift required for sustainable automation.

## Audience

- **Primary:** Network Infrastructure Leaders, Engineering Managers, Transformation Leads
- **Secondary:** Engineers navigating their own career evolution

## Content Outline

### Change Management
- Reduce resistance, build excitement, create ownership
- Preserve critical institutional knowledge
- Transparent communication throughout

### Getting People Onboard
- Early involvement of engineers in design decisions
- Demonstrate quick wins — painkillers, not vitamins
- Internal champions / ambassador program
- Automation Ambassador model: early adopters with genuine influence

#### Messaging Themes
- "Automation removes toil, not jobs"
- "Engineers evolve, not get replaced"
- "Opportunities to learn high-value skills"

### Building Excitement
- Show tangible before/after improvements
- Highlight success stories from within the organisation
- Gamify adoption (metrics, milestones, recognition)
- Provide structured learning pathways
- AI as a learning partner for engineers
- Extends: [Using AI as a Learning Partner](https://ppklau.github.io/article/using_ai_as_a_learning_partner/)

### Skills Transformation
- Existing network engineers evolve toward: automation usage, scripting, pipeline understanding
- Advanced roles: Automation Engineer (bridge role), Network Automation Architect
- Deep networking expertise remains essential — automation amplifies, not replaces

### Hiring vs Upskilling Strategy

#### Upskill When
- Strong networking SME base exists
- Institutional knowledge is critical
- Cultural continuity matters

#### Hire When
- Software/DevOps skills are missing entirely
- Need to accelerate transformation timeline
- Require architectural leadership for new capabilities

### The "A-Team" Framework
- Senior Leadership: set direction, remove obstacles, manage risk
- Product Management: translate technical decisions to business outcomes
- Network Architecture: institutional memory, design authority, risk management
- Network Engineering: strategy to production, continuous improvement
- Trust as the binding force
- Extends: [AI and Network Automation article](https://ppklau.github.io/article/ai_and_network_automation_for_finance_leaders/)

### Critical Balance

> Do not lose networking expertise while chasing automation.

- Retain key SMEs
- Pair SMEs with automation engineers
- Build cross-functional teams

### Organisational Model Evolution
- From: siloed network teams
- To: cross-functional platform teams (Network + Automation + DevOps)
- Platform ownership model: who maintains the automation platform?

### Architecture Decision Records
- Document transformation decisions with rationale
- Preserve context for future team members
- Avoid repeated debates on settled architectural choices
